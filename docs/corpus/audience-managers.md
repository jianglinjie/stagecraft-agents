# Writing for managers and decision makers

## Outcomes first
Managers read to decide, so open with the outcome and what it changes for the team: time saved, risk removed, cost avoided. Mechanism and detail come after, for the readers who want them.

## Quantify cost and effort
State what adoption costs in people, weeks and money, and what it replaces. Ranges are fine when they are honest; an unexplained single number invites distrust.

## Name the risks
A short risks section builds more trust than a long benefits list. For each risk give its likelihood, its impact and the mitigation. Leaving risks out makes the piece read like an advertisement.

## Keep it to one page of substance
Aim for a summary a busy reader can finish in three minutes. Move tables, methodology and appendices behind links or to the end.
